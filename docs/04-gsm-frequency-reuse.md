# GSM Frequency Reuse and Cellular Network Planning

## Overview

This module focuses on the mathematical analysis and optimization of GSM cellular networks through frequency reuse planning and interference evaluation. Since radio spectrum is a limited resource, cellular systems must reuse frequencies across geographically separated cells while maintaining acceptable communication quality.

The project implements several MATLAB simulations to evaluate regular hexagonal cellular layouts and irregular network topologies. Different frequency reuse factors and propagation environments are analyzed to study their impact on Carrier-to-Interference Ratio (CIR), network capacity, and overall service quality. In addition, a Game Theory-based channel allocation algorithm is implemented to optimize spectrum utilization in irregular deployments.

---

# Objectives

The main objectives of this module are:

- Simulate GSM cellular networks using regular and irregular topologies.
- Analyze the impact of the frequency reuse factor on co-channel interference.
- Evaluate Carrier-to-Interference Ratio (CIR) under different propagation environments.
- Study the influence of the propagation exponent on network performance.
- Compare random frequency allocation against Game Theory-based optimization.
- Estimate network quality using voice and data performance indicators.

---

# Cellular Network Fundamentals

Unlike local wireless networks, cellular systems divide large geographical areas into multiple cells that reuse the same frequency spectrum. This strategy dramatically increases network capacity while minimizing the amount of radio spectrum required.

However, frequency reuse introduces co-channel interference, where signals transmitted on the same frequency interfere with one another. The primary objective of cellular planning is therefore to maximize spectral efficiency while maintaining acceptable communication quality across the entire coverage area.

---

# Frequency Reuse Theory

Frequency reuse is controlled by the reuse factor (*k*), which determines how frequently the same frequency channel may be assigned throughout the network.

Increasing the reuse factor enlarges the distance between co-channel cells, reducing interference and improving signal quality. Conversely, smaller reuse factors increase system capacity but also generate higher interference levels.

The simulations also evaluate different propagation exponents (*γ*), representing various radio propagation environments. Higher propagation exponents produce faster signal attenuation, significantly reducing interference generated by distant cells.

# MATLAB Simulation Environment

All simulations were developed using MATLAB.

The repository includes several scripts responsible for different stages of the analysis:

- `channelallocation.m`
- `reuse.m`
- `PRUEBA.m`
- `rng.m`

These programs generate cellular layouts, assign frequency channels, compute propagation losses, evaluate Carrier-to-Interference Ratios, and visualize the obtained results.

---

# Regular Cellular Network Model

The first scenario models a regular hexagonal GSM deployment composed of 50 cells with identical coverage areas.

For every simulated configuration, the system computes the spatial distribution of Carrier-to-Interference Ratio (CIR), allowing the evaluation of signal quality across the entire network.

Performance is assessed using statistical percentiles (5th, 50th and 95th), providing insight into average users as well as worst-case cell-edge conditions.

---

# Carrier-to-Interference Ratio Analysis

Several combinations of frequency reuse factors and propagation exponents were evaluated.

The simulations demonstrate that increasing either the reuse factor or the propagation exponent significantly improves Carrier-to-Interference Ratio by reducing the influence of co-channel interference.

The lowest performance is obtained when using aggressive frequency reuse together with low propagation losses, where interference dominates the received signal.

Conversely, larger reuse factors and higher attenuation environments produce substantially cleaner radio conditions and improved communication quality.

# Cell Radius Evaluation

An additional experiment evaluated the influence of cell radius while maintaining constant reuse parameters.

Cell sizes ranging from 2 km to 15 km were simulated.

The obtained results demonstrate that Carrier-to-Interference Ratio remains practically unchanged when the reuse factor is fixed. Since both serving and interfering cells scale proportionally, the signal-to-interference relationship remains nearly constant regardless of absolute cell size.

# Game Theory Channel Allocation

Real cellular deployments rarely follow perfectly regular geometries.

To model realistic scenarios, an irregular network composed of randomly distributed cells was generated.

Instead of assigning frequencies randomly, the project implements a decentralized Game Theory algorithm in which every cell independently selects the channel that minimizes local interference while considering neighboring cells.

The optimization process converges after only a few iterations, producing a significantly improved frequency distribution compared with purely random channel allocation.

The optimized deployments are illustrated in:

- `cir-heatmap-irregular-k3.png`
- `cir-heatmap-irregular-k5.png`
- `cir-heatmap-irregular-k7.png`
- `cir-heatmap-irregular-k9.png`

These simulations demonstrate how increasing the number of available frequency groups progressively reduces co-channel interference throughout the network.

# Performance Evaluation

The calculated Carrier-to-Interference Ratio values were translated into practical GSM service quality indicators.

Particular attention was given to the 5th percentile, representing users located near cell boundaries where interference is most severe.

The results show that:

- Higher reuse factors significantly improve service reliability.
- Higher propagation exponents reduce co-channel interference.
- Better CIR values directly translate into higher voice quality (MOS) and increased GPRS throughput.
- Poor CIR values produce unstable communications and degraded user experience at cell edges.

---

# Design Decisions

Several engineering decisions were adopted throughout the project:

**MATLAB-Based Simulation**

MATLAB was selected due to its powerful numerical computation capabilities and visualization tools for wireless communication studies.

**Percentile-Based Evaluation**

Using statistical percentiles provides a more realistic assessment than relying solely on average values, especially for worst-case coverage scenarios.

**Game Theory Optimization**

A decentralized Game Theory algorithm was implemented to simulate intelligent spectrum allocation in irregular cellular deployments.

**Heatmap Visualization**

Spatial heatmaps were selected as the primary visualization method because they clearly reveal interference regions and coverage quality across the simulated area.
